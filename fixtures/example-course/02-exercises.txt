Exercise sheet 1: A fair die and indicator variables

These exercises and solutions are synthetic material written for this repository.

Exercise 1
Roll a fair six-sided die once. Let A = {2, 4, 6} and B = {4, 5, 6}.
Find P(A | B), and decide whether A and B are independent.

Solution
P(A) = 3/6 = 1/2 and P(B) = 3/6 = 1/2.
A intersect B = {4, 6}, so P(A intersect B) = 2/6 = 1/3.
Since P(B) > 0, P(A | B) = (1/3)/(1/2) = 2/3.
The events are not independent because 1/3 differs from (1/2)(1/2) = 1/4.

Exercise 2
Let X be Bernoulli with parameter p = 0.3 and let Y = X.
Find E[X + Y]. Does applying linearity require independence here?

Solution
E[X + Y] = E[X] + E[Y] = 0.3 + 0.3 = 0.6.
Linearity does not require independence. In this example X and Y are dependent:
P(X = 1 and Y = 1) = 0.3, whereas P(X = 1)P(Y = 1) = 0.09.

Learning goals
Keep conditioning assumptions explicit. Test independence with its definition.
Distinguish linearity of expectation from rules that require independence.
